A quiet luxury watch is not the one with the biggest logo. It is the one with a real story behind it — a design that has survived decades without needing to change.

Here are three, from Swiss automatic to a digital icon. Each one starts a conversation among people who know watches, and says nothing to people who do not.

01Tissot PRX Powermatic 80, Ice Blue

Swiss automatic, 1970s shape

The PRX brings back a Tissot case first made in 1978: flat, with an integrated steel bracelet and a waffle-textured ice blue dial. Inside, the Powermatic 80 automatic movement keeps running for about 80 hours — take it off on Friday and it is still ticking on Monday.

The detail worth knowing: P and R stand for Precise and Robust, and the X is the Roman numeral ten — for 10 bar, or 100 metres, of water resistance.

Best for: the one serious watch — for work, weekends and handing down.

02Timex Marlin Hand-Wound, 1960s Reissue

A small morning ritual on your wrist

The Marlin is a faithful reissue of Timex's own 1960s dress watch: 34 mm, a domed crystal and a silver dial with elegant numerals. It has no battery and no rotor — you wind the crown by hand, and a full wind gives about 40 hours.

Why owners love it: winding it each morning, at the same time, becomes a quiet ritual. It is as close to a genuine vintage dress watch as you can get without inheriting one.

Best for: tailoring, weddings and anyone who wants a real mechanical watch.

03Casio A168WG-9 Vintage Gold

The flex of a man with nothing to prove

A gold Casio worn with a good jacket is pure confidence — it says you care about style but have nothing left to prove. It has a light, alarm and stopwatch, and the battery lasts about seven years.

The fact that surprises people: the A168 design first appeared in the late 1980s and won Japan's Good Design Long Life Design Award in 2011 — a prize for designs that stay relevant for decades.

Best for: a weekend watch, a gift under $100, or a second watch beside a serious one.

Start with one, wear it every day. A watch looks expensive when it looks lived-in and chosen — not when it is new.
